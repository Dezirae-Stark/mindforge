Safety notes — read before first use
  • Not a medical device. Nothing here diagnoses, treats, or cures anything, and none of it substitutes for medical or psychological care. The research sections describe findings in the literature; they do not establish that this implementation produces any outcome for any person.
  • Adults only. Intended for people 18 or over. Not for use by minors, and not to be run on a minor as a subject.
  • Never while driving or operating machinery. Do not use this while driving, cycling, operating machinery, or anywhere a lapse in attention carries a cost. These protocols are designed to reduce vigilance and can induce drowsiness without warning. Sit or lie down, somewhere safe.
  • Photosensitivity. Susceptibility to photosensitive seizure peaks at 15–25 Hz, within a broader susceptible range of 1–65 Hz (Fisher, Harding et al., Epilepsy Foundation of America Working Group, Epilepsia 46(9), 2005). Do not use any flashing or flickering mode if you have a history of seizures or photosensitivity, or if you do not know whether you do.
  • Mental health. Stop if you become distressed, disoriented, or dissociated. These protocols are not appropriate during acute psychiatric crisis, psychosis, mania, or active depersonalisation, and prolonged sensory reduction can intensify those states rather than settle them.
  • Headphone volume. Set the level before you start, at the quietest setting that works. Binaural material is worn for long stretches, and sustained exposure damages hearing at volumes that feel comfortable minute to minute.
  • Drowsiness at depth. Deep theta work reliably produces sleep onset and hypnagogic imagery. Expect to lose time. Do not schedule anything immediately afterward that requires you to be sharp, and give yourself several minutes to reorient before standing up.

Session notes
The Radin Protocol
Dean Radin's IONS presentiment experiments (2004–2023) measured electrodermal activity continuously while participants viewed randomly-selected images. Physiological arousal rose measurably 1–4 seconds BEFORE emotional stimuli were displayed — before any conscious knowledge of which type was coming (double-blind random selection).

Effect size: d = 0.21  ·  p < 2.7 × 10⁻¹²  ·  26 studies across 20 independent laboratories.

This trainer runs a subjective analogue: you report your gut-sense (calm / mild / activated) before each reveal. A "correct" presentiment is calm before neutral, activated before emotional. Mild counts as partial (0.5 pts) regardless of valence. Chance baseline for correct guesses averages 0.5 pts per trial.
