# Remote Viewing — Citation Record

Bibliographic records for `remote-viewing.html`'s research basis, per the Prior-Art Protocol in `CLAUDE.md`. Every entry must be independently verifiable (author, year, venue, DOI/link where available) before being cited in the tool itself.

## Core protocol sources

- Targ, R., & Puthoff, H. (1974). Information transmission under conditions of sensory shielding. *Nature*, 251, 602–607. — the founding SRI coordinate-RV result.
- Utts, J. (1996). An assessment of the evidence for psychic functioning. *Journal of Scientific Exploration*, 10(1), 3–30. (Also *Journal of Parapsychology*, 59(4), 289–320. Frequently miscited as "1995, JSE 9(4), 351–396" — that volume/page range does not exist; 1995 is the date of the underlying AIR report.) — meta-analysis of the SRI/SAIC data, d≈0.40 for trained viewers.
- SAIC Stargate program declassified material (general) — see the cross-repository entry below; primary sources for this category are now held directly rather than cited at one remove.

## Cross-repository reference: SRI/STARGATE program primary documents (Puthoff/Targ 1978, Kress 1977, AIR 1995)

**Added 2026-07-29, per operator approval.** Cross-reference to a compartment in the operator's private research repository, which holds the full digest and the primary-source PDFs. The repository and compartment path are deliberately not recorded here.

**What it is:** three primary documents fetched and read directly, giving this tool's existing secondhand citations (Targ & Puthoff 1974; Utts 1995) actual primary-source backing:
1. **Puthoff, H.E. & Targ, R., "Standard Remote-Viewing Protocol (Local Targets)," SRI International, November 1978** — the actual working protocol this tool's 6-stage CRV process descends from. Specifies target-pool construction (distinctive-but-not-distinct-from-each-other targets, sealed in randomized numbered envelopes), subject-selection criteria ("self-confident, uninhibited, successful, and not afraid to be wrong" — found more reliable than any psychological test the authors tried), and explicitly **rejects** hypnosis, strobe lights, or sensory-deprivation procedures as part of the protocol ("we are in this sense proponents of a 'naturalist school'"). **If this tool's implementation is ever compared against the source protocol for fidelity, use this document as the baseline**, not a secondary description of it.
2. **Kress, K.A., "Parapsychology in Intelligence: A Personal Review and Conclusions," *Studies in Intelligence*, Winter 1977** — the CIA's own Project Officer's internal, contemporaneous account. His own concluding assessment: "a skeptical agnostic," not an advocate — a useful corrective against both over-crediting and dismissing the program's internal culture.
3. **Mumford, M.D., Rose, A.M., & Goslin, D.A. (American Institutes for Research), "An Evaluation of Remote Viewing: Research and Applications," September 29, 1995** — the actual primary source behind the "Utts (1995)" citation above. **Important precision this compartment's digest establishes:** the widely-circulated "psychic functioning has been well established" line is from Dr. Utts's *individual* chapter, not the panel's joint Executive Summary. The joint summary's actual finding is narrower: a statistically anomalous laboratory effect whose paranormal origin was explicitly not established ("the laboratory experiments have not identified the origins or nature of the remote viewing phenomenon, if, indeed, it exists at all"), and — the finding that actually ended the program — unanimous agreement that it produced no actionable intelligence in real operational use. **Any future citation of this report in this tool's own materials should use the joint-summary language, not the more one-sided Utts-only soundbite that circulates in secondary sources.**

## Cross-repository reference: LaMothe, "Controlled Offensive Behavior — USSR" (DIA, 1972)

**Added 2026-07-28, per operator approval.** Cross-reference to a compartment in the operator's private research repository, which holds the full digest and the primary-source document. The repository and compartment path are deliberately not recorded here.

**What it is:** A declassified DIA report (Capt. John D. LaMothe, Medical Service Corps; DIA Short Title ST-CS-01-169-72; published July 1972; SECRET/No Foreign Dissemination until declassified 1990) surveying alleged Soviet research into parapsychology and behavior manipulation. Authenticity independently cross-verified against the CIA's own FOIA reading room, the DIA's official FOIA electronic reading room, and an independent Internet Archive mirror — this is a real, genuinely declassified document, not a hoax or a fabrication.

**Why it's relevant here:** this is a documented **precursor** to the SRI/SAIC program this tool's own research basis already cites. Remote-viewing historians credit reports like this one with helping convince the U.S. intelligence community that the strategic risk of a Soviet "psi gap" — regardless of whether the underlying phenomena were real — justified funding its own investigation, which became the SRI coordinate-RV program (Targ & Puthoff, 1974) and later SAIC/Stargate. It documents the institutional and strategic context that led directly to the protocol this tool implements, one link further back in the citation chain than Targ/Puthoff/Utts alone reach.

**What it is NOT, and should not be cited as:**
- **Not a scientific validation of ESP or psychokinesis.** The 1972 report's own named claims (principally Dr. L.L. Vasilev's telepathy research and Nina Kulagina's psychokinesis demonstrations) are Soviet-side claims relayed for U.S. threat-assessment purposes, not independently adjudicated findings. The the private research repository compartment's digest flags this explicitly: Kulagina's psychokinesis claims in particular have a well-documented, still-unresolved history of skeptical investigation (sleight-of-hand hypotheses from magicians and skeptical investigators, a 1987 *Pravda* fraud accusation and defamation suit that ended in a partial judgment with no physical evidence of fraud ever produced either) — a history the 1972 report predates and does not address.
- **Not itself a STARGATE program document.** STARGATE began later (~1978); this report is 1972, part of the earlier justification period.
- **Not a citation for this tool's own effect-size claims.** Targ & Puthoff (1974) and Utts (1995) remain the operative evidentiary citations for coordinate-RV effect sizes; this document's role is institutional/historical context for *why* the program existed, not additional evidence for *whether remote viewing works*.
