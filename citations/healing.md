# Remote Healing — Citation Record

Bibliographic records for `healing.html`'s research basis, per the Prior-Art Protocol in `CLAUDE.md`. Every entry below was verified during the citation audit of 2026-08-05 / 2026-08-08 against Crossref, PubMed, or the publisher of record. Nothing here is cited from memory.

**Verification key:** ✅ verified against Crossref/PubMed · 📄 verified against publisher or archive copy · ⚠️ exists but the finding is disputed or failed replication · ❌ claim not supported by any source found

## Distant-healing trials

- ⚠️ **Sicher, F., Targ, E., Moore, D., & Smith, H.S. (1998).** A randomized double-blind study of the effect of distant healing in a population with advanced AIDS: report of a small scale study. *Western Journal of Medicine*, 169(6), 356–363. PMID 9866433 · PMC1305403.
  **Note the first author is Sicher, not Targ** — this study is widely miscited as "Targ et al."
  **Disputed.** Journalistic and skeptical investigation subsequently alleged that the primary outcome measures were changed after unblinding, when the pre-registered endpoint showed no effect. This citation must never appear without that caveat attached.

- ⚠️ **Astin, J.A., Harkness, E., & Ernst, E. (2000).** The efficacy of "distant healing": a systematic review of randomized trials. *Annals of Internal Medicine*, 132(11), 903–910. doi:[10.7326/0003-4819-132-11-200006060-00009](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-132-11-200006060-00009)
  23 trials, 2,774 patients. **The authors' conclusion is equivocal, not positive:** methodological limitations across the included studies made definitive conclusions about efficacy impossible. Do not cite this as supportive.

- 📄 **Braud, W., & Schlitz, M. (1991).** Consciousness interactions with remote biological systems: anomalous intentionality effects. *Subtle Energies*, 2(1), 1–46. No Crossref DOI.
  Archive: https://journals.sfu.ca/seemj/index.php/seemj/article/view/112

- 📄 **Bengston, W.F., & Krinsley, D. (2000).** The effect of the "laying on of hands" on transplanted breast cancer in mice. *Journal of Scientific Exploration*, 14(3), 353–364. No Crossref DOI.
  Source of the image-cycling method used by the manifestation overlay. ⚠️ An extraordinary claim (reported remission in a 100%-fatal mouse model) resting on small-n work from a single research line. It has not been independently replicated by a laboratory outside that line. Cite it as the origin of the technique, not as established biology.

## Scope — this is the tool where clinical-claim discipline matters most

The charter forbids clinical claims, and this tool's subject matter sits closest to them. Two invariants:

1. **Nothing in this tool treats, cures, or affects any medical condition**, and no citation above establishes that it does. The strongest honest statement the literature supports is that the question has been studied and the results are contested and methodologically weak.
2. **Consent is required.** This is a two-person protocol directed at a named third party who may not be present. The tool must never encourage running a session on someone who has not agreed. The consent item is part of the shared safety block.

⚠️ The target's name and location are entered into the UI. They are held in session state only and are **not** written to `localStorage` — verified during the 2026-08-05 audit. Keep it that way: a stored name-plus-location record about a third party is a different privacy proposition from a personal practice log.
