# Solfeggio — Citation Record

Bibliographic records for `solfeggio.html`'s research basis, per the Prior-Art Protocol in `CLAUDE.md`. Every entry below was verified during the citation audit of 2026-08-05 / 2026-08-08 against Crossref, PubMed, or the publisher of record. Nothing here is cited from memory.

**Verification key:** ✅ verified against Crossref/PubMed · 📄 verified against publisher or archive copy · ⚠️ exists but the finding is disputed or failed replication · ❌ claim not supported by any source found

## Summary: this tool has no research basis, and the README should say so

Unlike every other tool in the suite, the Solfeggio frequency set has **no primary literature**. A search for peer-reviewed support for the specific frequencies, their assigned effects, or their durations returned nothing usable. This record exists to document that absence honestly, per the charter's "honest absence over confident filler" commitment.

## Actual provenance of the frequency set

- 📄 **Puleo, J., & Horowitz, L.G. (1999).** *Healing Codes for the Biological Apocalypse.* Tetrahedron Publishing Group.
  This is the origin of the modern Solfeggio set — not an ancient tradition. In the 1970s Joseph Puleo, a naturopath, reported being intuitively directed to the Book of Numbers, and applied Pythagorean digit-reduction to Numbers 7:12–83 to extract six repeating numbers: **396, 417, 528, 639, 741, 852**. Horowitz co-published and popularised them. **174, 285 and 963 Hz were added later** by extending the same numerical pattern.

- ❌ **There is no medieval or Gregorian pedigree.** Medieval-music scholarship holds that neither the pitch relationships nor the specific frequencies of the modern Solfeggio set can be traced to Gregorian chant. The hexachord syllables (Ut–Re–Mi–Fa–Sol–La) from *Ut queant laxis* are real and are Guido of Arezzo's; they carry no Hertz values. The hertz did not exist as a unit until the 19th century. The two things are unrelated.

- ❌ **432 Hz** is a separate tuning controversy with its own (also unsupported) claims, and does not belong to the Puleo set at all. It appears in this tool's sequence regardless.

## Claims in the tool that are not supportable

The tone table currently labels frequencies with therapeutic effects. These are **marketing labels inherited from the Puleo/Horowitz lineage, not findings**, and two of them are clinical claims of the kind the charter's anti-pattern list forbids:

| Label in tool | Status |
|---|---|
| 174 Hz "Removes Pain" | ❌ Clinical claim. No supporting evidence located. |
| 285 Hz "Influences Energy Field" | ❌ No defined referent; not testable as stated. |
| 396 / 417 / 639 / 741 / 852 / 963 Hz effects | ❌ No supporting evidence located for any. |
| 528 Hz "Repairs DNA" | ❌ Clinical/biological claim. Systematic searches of PubMed, Web of Science and Google Scholar return no controlled reproducible study showing any audible frequency repairs DNA in living cells. The nearest real work (e.g. a 528 Hz vs 440 Hz astrocyte cell-culture study reporting reduced cell death under ethanol stress) does not demonstrate DNA repair and does not generalise to a person listening to a tone. |
| "each played for its **research-specified** duration" | ❌ Asserts that research specifies these durations. No such research was located. The durations are editorial choices. |

## What *is* defensible about this tool

- ✅ The **binaural-beat layer** is real signal processing on the same footing as the rest of the suite — see `citations/mindforge.md`, Oster (1973), doi:[10.1038/scientificamerican1073-94](https://doi.org/10.1038/scientificamerican1073-94).
- ✅ The **photosensitive safety modal** rests on Fisher et al. (2005), *Epilepsia* 46(9), 1426–1441, doi:[10.1111/j.1528-1167.2005.31405.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1528-1167.2005.31405.x).
- Playing a sustained pure tone at a chosen pitch for a set period is a perfectly legitimate contemplative practice. **It needs no scientific justification and should not be given a false one.**

## Recommendation (operator decision — not actioned)

Rename the tone labels to neutral descriptors (pitch and traditional name only), delete "research-specified", and add a Research basis section stating the Puleo/Horowitz provenance plainly. The frequencies and the practice can stay exactly as they are; only the claims attached to them need to go. As it stands this tool is the suite's single largest deviation from its own charter — it makes clinical claims, and it is the only tool with no Research basis section at all.
